### 0.1 The seven questions of prompt §6

**1 — What does `BREAK_POINT_ALL` change, and what does the version-2 grid change?** Separated
rather than confounded, at the production setting, in §3. **The policy changes nothing on two of
the three models and a little on the third**: `RadiationModel` and `LambdaCDMModel` declare no
break points, so `all` and `discontinuity` take the same code path and every figure is *identical*,
not merely close. On `QCDModel` the policy moves the count above 3e-6 from 9 to 6 on the version-0
grid and from 7 to 4 on the version-2 grid, leaves the worst excursion unmoved (3.02e-4 and 2.71e-4
respectively), and costs +0.82 % / +0.61 % of the sector's evaluations. **The grid change is the
larger of the two**: version 0 → version 2 at fixed policy takes Radiation from 3 wavenumbers above
target to 1, LambdaCDM from 13 to 9 and its worst from **8.64e-4 to 3.36e-4**, and QCD from 9 to 7.
Together, `[12-tk-numeric-atol-largest-k-excursion]`'s **3 / 13 / 8** becomes **1 / 9 / 4** and its
worst **8.64e-4** becomes **3.36e-4**. The issue does not disappear; it shrinks by about a third.

Prompt 17's own configuration reproduces exactly where it can: version-0 per-$k$ under
`discontinuity` gives **3 / 13** wavenumbers above target and **8.64e-4** worst on Radiation and
LambdaCDM, which are prompt 17's published figures to three figures. QCD gives **9**, not prompt

$T(z)$ representation after prompt 17 measured it, so the QCD column is a figure on a different
background and only the two smooth models are a construction check.

**2 — Is the error set by `rtol`, and does `atol = 1e-13` bind?** **`rtol` sets the level; `atol`
does not set it but chooses which wavenumbers excurse, and that is not the same statement as prompt
03's "`atol` is inert".** Holding `rtol = 1e-8` and moving `atol` across two decades,
`1e-12 → 1e-14`, moves the *median of the per-$k$ maxima* by at most a factor of 2.1 on any model
(5.93e-7 → 2.83e-7 on Radiation, 5.83e-7 → 4.22e-7 on LambdaCDM, 8.06e-7 → 6.18e-7 on QCD) while
moving the *maximum* by up to **205x** (3.07e-4 → 1.50e-6 on Radiation) and moving the wavenumber at
which it falls. Moving `rtol` across five decades at fixed `atol = 1e-13` moves the median from
2.01e-5 to 2.27e-8, a factor of **885**, monotonically. So README §2 (e)'s magnitude argument does
*not* transfer from the $G_k$ sector: $T$ starts at 1 and decays, `atol = 1e-13` is a real
constraint on the solver's step selection, and what it buys is not accuracy but a different draw
from the same distribution of step sequences. That is prompt 17 §7's conclusion, confirmed on the
version-2 grid under the production policy and across the whole $k$ grid rather than at one
wavenumber per model.

**3 — README §6.1's target rule, applied.** The floor is the initial-condition truncation, freshly
measured in §5 at **2.39e-6 to 2.64e-6** of the envelope over 150 (model, $k$) runs against the
inherited 2.52e-6 — it survives the grid and policy change, as an initial-condition truncation
should, and the conservative reading takes the smallest, 2.39e-6. §4.1 sweeps loose to tight:
`1e-6`, `1e-7`, `1e-8`, `3e-9`, `1e-9`, `3e-10` and `1e-10` all miss; **`3e-11` is the first that
clears**, at 3.88e-8 worst over three models, and `1e-11` clears too. Rule 3 says the first, so the
recommendation is **`3e-11`** and not `1e-11`, which buys nothing and costs 2.0 % more.

**The rule's answer rests on a non-monotone maximum and the recommendation must be read with that
attached.** From `1e-9` down to `1e-10` exactly **one** of the 150 runs sits above target at each
setting, and it is a *different* run each time: `1.894e6` on QCD at `1e-9` (9.08e-4), `4.972e7` on
QCD at `3e-10` (5.76e-4), `5.943e6` on LambdaCDM at `1e-10` (1.31e-4). The maximum is therefore not
a decreasing function of `rtol` and "`3e-11` clears" is an observation over 150 runs, not a bound
over the sector. It is the same phenomenon prompt 17 §7 (b) demonstrated by perturbing $k$: an
occasional step sequence mis-resolves the first oscillations as the mode enters the horizon and
carries the error to the end of the run. `[03a-tk-numeric-excursion-is-sporadic-in-rtol]` records
it.

**4 — What does it cost?** §6, in right-hand-side evaluations, times the sector's object count.
`TkNumericIntegration` is **one object per wavenumber, 50 per model** (`main.py:1215`, inside the
$k$ loop alone). At the recommended `3e-11` the three models together go from **1,272,891** to
**1,774,977** evaluations, **+39.4 %**; one step looser (`1e-10`) is +35.7 % and one step tighter
(`1e-11`) is +42.2 %. Per model the increase is +44.8 % / +37.2 % / +36.7 %. Nothing in README
§4.3's factor-of-two stop is approached, and the absolute number is three orders below the $G_k$
sector's.

**5 — What binds `_solve_horizon_exit`, and to what?** §7.3: the `rtol` term, everywhere, at the
production setting — `xtol` binds at **0 of 150** (k, offset) pairs on each model, because
$r_{\rm tol}|u|$ runs 2.06e-7 to 3.80e-7 against $x_{\rm tol} = 1e-10$. It does bind further down
the axis, and the measurement says exactly where: at `xtol = 1e-10` it takes over at
`rtol` $\lesssim 2.6\times10^{-12}$, so the pair floors at **1e-10** in $u$ however far `rtol` is
tightened alone. The worst achieved displacement at the production setting is **6.18e-8**
(LambdaCDM) and **7.86e-8** (QCD) and **exactly 0** on the radiation control, where $q$ is linear in
$u$ and Brent lands on the root. The residual $|q|$ tracks the displacement and is 7 orders below
`DEFAULT_HEXIT_TOLERANCE = 1e-2`, which is a post-hoc guard and not a tolerance. The $u\to z$
recovery never competes: one ulp of a double $u$ at these magnitudes is **7.11e-15** relative in
$1+z$, the production criterion stands **5.3e7** times above it and even the tightest pin in the
matrix stands 53 times above it — and the tightest achieved displacement is 7.11e-15, i.e. exactly
one ulp, so the root is pinned to the limit of the representation before the conversion is reached.

**6 — The target rule, or rule 6.** **Both, because there are two consumers and only one of them
admits a tolerance.** Against the row match, `DEFAULT_REDSHIFT_RELATIVE_PRECISION = 1e-7`, a floor
*can* be established and the rule applies: the loosest `rtol` whose guarantee clears it is **`1e-9`**
(§7.6). Against the grid digest, which is bit-identity, **no floor could be established, therefore
no target** — §7.5 shows the digest turning over at a 1e-14 relative anchor shift on all three
models, four orders below the tightest pin available, so no `(xtol, rtol)` makes the tag
reproducible and none is proposed. The second half is prompt 05's design tolerance, not a tolerance
value this prompt may choose.

**7 — What would the decoupled pairs be?** §0 above, and the log's "State handed to the next
prompt" carries the five provenance fields of README §1.2 for each half:
`DEFAULT_TK_NUMERIC_REL_TOLERANCE = 3e-11` and `DEFAULT_TK_NUMERIC_ABS_TOLERANCE = 1e-13`
(unchanged, settled 2026-09-12, and measured here to be a step-selection knob rather than an

## 7. `wavenumber_exit_time` (§6 question 5)

Taken through `CosmologyConcepts.wavenumber._solve_horizon_exit` directly and **never through the datastore**: that target's lookup accepts any stored row at least as tight as the request and returns the loosest such, so a loosened sweep point would be served a tighter stored row and the object would report the stored pair (`[02-wavenumber-exit-time-tolerance-is-an-inequality-key]`). All fifty production wavenumbers, three models, and the three offsets a grid is built from: horizon crossing itself, $z_{\rm exit,suph,e5}$ (the source grid's anchor) and $z_{\rm exit,subh,e4}$ (where the $G_k$ numeric region stops).

**The error measure is the displacement in $u = \log(1+z)$**, which is where the root lives and is also the *relative* displacement in $1+z$, since $d(1+z)/(1+z) = du$. That is what makes it an accuracy for a location rather than for a value: a misplaced root moves a grid by a relative amount. The reference is a converged re-solve at `(1e-300, 1e-14)` -- the pair `_solve_T_z` already uses -- scored against `(1e-300, 1e-15)` through the same `reference_drift` the ODE sectors use.
